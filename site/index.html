What it is

Your records, on the air.

You own a music collection worth listening to properly. Booth programs it like a good radio station would: it picks songs that belong together, in an order that flows, and a DJ comes on between them to tell you something true about what you just heard.

01 · Ask

"Sunday morning coffee, an hour."

Describe a show on your phone or your computer: a mood, a drive, a decade, an artist you've been meaning to revisit. Pick a length, from 20 minutes to 4 hours, and a DJ.

02 · Booth makes it

Booth chooses from the songs you own and writes the breaks, the bits where the DJ talks: a story about the band, why two songs sit side by side, the year you added an album. Every fact is checked.

03 · Listen

The show arrives as a playlist in Plex, or in a shows folder for your music player. You can also make it one MP3 to take on a drive. Play it whenever you like.

Your music stays home. Booth runs on the computer with your music. When you ask for a show, it sends only the details of your library (titles, artists, albums, years and the like) so the DJ can plan, and that isn't kept afterwards. Your music files never leave your computer, and nothing on the internet can connect in to it. The full details.

Install

Put Booth on the computer with your music.

That's the Windows PC or Mac where Plex runs, or where your music folder lives. You do this once; after that Booth starts by itself and updates itself.

Windows Your computer

Download for Windows
  1. Your browser may warn you. Booth is new, so it may say the file "isn't commonly downloaded". It's safe to keep:
    • Chrome: click Keep (or Download suspicious file).
    • Edge: click the … next to the download, then Keep, Show more and Keep anyway.
  2. Double-click RadioBooth-Windows.exe in your Downloads.
  3. If a blue box says "Windows protected your PC", click More info, then Run anyway. Windows says this about any new app it hasn't seen much yet.

Windows 10 or 11.

Mac Your computer

Download for Mac
  1. Find Radio Booth in your Downloads. Safari unzips it for you; with another browser, double-click RadioBooth-macOS.zip first.
  2. Double-click Radio Booth. The first time, your Mac says it can't check it for malicious software, because Booth isn't registered with Apple yet. Click Done.
  3. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down, click Open Anyway next to "Radio Booth", and confirm with your password. (macOS 11 or 12: System Preferences › Security & Privacy.)

Booth moves itself into Applications, so you can delete the download. Any Mac from macOS 11 (Big Sur) on, Apple silicon or Intel.

When Booth opens, your browser shows Booth's page. That page lives only on this computer, and it's the whole app: there's no other window. Leave it open for the next part.

Plex on a NAS, Linux, or another computer? See other ways to set up Booth.

Your first show

From "sign in" to "on air".

You'll use two pages: Booth's page on your computer, and your Booth page on your phone. Here's the whole trip.

  1. Sign in on your phone

    Open the sign-in link from your invitation on your phone. That's your Booth page. Add it to your home screen (in the Share menu on iPhone, or the ⋮ menu on Android) so it's one tap away. The link is yours alone, so please don't pass it on.

  2. Tell Booth where your music is

    On Booth's page on the computer, pick one. Not sure which? See the difference.

    It's in Plex

    Click It's in Plex. Plex's own sign-in opens: sign in with the account that owns your Plex server and allow Radio Booth. Booth never sees your password. Then come back to Booth's tab and pick your music library if you have more than one.

    It's in a folder

    Click It's in a folder, then Use these folders (Booth suggests your music folder and a shows folder; change them if needed). Booth reads your music's tags with a progress bar. A big collection takes a while the first time; you can carry on meanwhile.

  3. Pair your phone and your computer

    On your phone, tap Get a pairing code. Type the code into Booth's page on the computer and click Connect. (The code works once, for 10 minutes.) The light at the top of Booth's page turns red and says On air. You're ready. You can close that browser tab now: Booth keeps running in the background.

  4. Ask for a show

    On your phone (or Booth's page on the computer), fill in the New show form and tap Make the show.

    What's the show?

    Rainy drive home, something warm

    Length

    40 min

    DJ

    Pick a personality

    Voice

    Pick a voice

    Station name

    Porch FM
    Make the show

    Describe it however you'd tell a friend. A few ideas to start with:

    • Sunday morning coffee
    • Songs from the year I finished school
    • Loud guitars for cleaning the garage
    • A slow fade into bedtime
    • Deep cuts from my most-played bands

    Every DJ works with every voice, so mix and match. You can give your DJ a name of your own, and name your station: the DJ says it on air. The page shows how the show is coming along; most take a few minutes.

  5. Play it

    With Plex, when your phone says In Plex, open Plex or Plexamp and look under Playlists. With a folder, it says In your Booth shows folder: open the new playlist there in your music player.

    To take a show along, use the buttons under it on Booth's computer page: Make one file mixes it into a single MP3, crossfaded like the radio, with a chapter for each song and break; Copy to a folder makes a folder of numbered files that plays in order on a phone, USB stick or car stereo. Remove deletes a show you're done with.

  6. Tell us what you think

    This is the part that helps most. Under each show on your phone, tap How was it? to give it stars and a note. If the DJ got something wrong, said a name oddly or went on too long, find that break in the list and tap Flag this break. Every rating and flag gets read.

Plex or folder

Two ways to keep your music. Both make great shows.

If your music is already in Plex, use Plex: you get the most out of the DJ and can listen anywhere. If you play files from a folder in a music player, the folder option is made for you.

PlexFolder
Where shows arrive A playlist in Plex, named after the show. The DJ's breaks go into a small library called Radio Booth that Booth adds, so they never mix with your music. A playlist file (.m3u8) plus the breaks, in your shows folder. Opens in foobar2000, MusicBee, VLC, Music on a Mac and most other players.
Listening away from home Anywhere Plex or Plexamp works for you, including your phone in the car. Plex downloads work too, for listening offline. Make one file and Copy to a folder work here as well. On the computer, or take it with you: Make one file gives a single MP3, and Copy to a folder gives numbered files for a phone, USB stick or car.
What the DJ knows Your tags plus what Plex remembers: your star ratings, how often you play things, and when you added them ("you've had this one since 2009"). What's in your files' tags: titles, artists, albums, years, genres. No ratings or added dates.
What you need Plex Media Server, signed in with the account that owns it. Booth works best on the computer that runs Plex. A server someone else shares with you won't work. A folder of music files (MP3, FLAC, M4A and the like) on this computer or a drive it can see. Booth reads them and never changes or moves them.
Sharing at home Add another person (they need their own Booth account) and they can ask for shows from your music too. Booth can share their playlists to their own Plex account. Add another person (they need their own Booth account) and they can ask for shows from your music too. Their shows land in the same shows folder, with their name on each.

You can switch later: on Booth's page, open Settings and click Change next to "Change Booth's setup".

Good to know

If something's off.

My show is stuck at "Waiting for your Booth Engineer"

That's Booth on your computer. It isn't running, or the computer is asleep. Wake the computer and open Radio Booth from the Start menu (Windows) or Applications (Mac). Booth's page should say On air, and the show carries on.

Does my computer have to stay on?

Only while a show is being made: it needs to be on and awake when you ask. Booth starts by itself when you sign in to the computer, so usually there's nothing to do. You can turn that off under Settings.

How do I get back to Booth's page on the computer?

Open Radio Booth from the Start menu (Windows), or from Applications, Launchpad or Spotlight (Mac). On a Mac you may see a note that a background item was added: that's Booth starting itself when you sign in.

A show says "Didn't work"

The reason is shown under the show. Try again; if it keeps happening, tell us what it says, and what Booth's page shows under What Booth has been doing.

Do I need to update Booth?

No. Booth checks for a new version every few hours and installs it when no show is on its way. Your version is shown under Settings on Booth's page.

Can I share the shows?

Shows are made from your own music for your own listening, on your own devices. To let someone at home make shows too, they need a Booth account of their own; then use Add another person under Settings.
